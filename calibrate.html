How the test works
  • Like at the optician: you see option 1, then option 2, in the same spot. Flip between them as often as you like with Space or the arrow keys, then choose.
  • Names and numbers stay hidden, and the order is random each time.
  • Judge by reading, not by first glance. Read a full line or two in each. Soft, low-contrast text looks calm for a second and then tires you; that's what went wrong with 1.2.0.
  • Say how sure you are: clearly when one wins at a glance, slightly when it's close. Keys 1 to 5, left to right.
  • Same is a real answer. It records which settings don't matter to you, and those stay at the research-backed defaults.
  • Six parts, about 15 minutes: the theme, its colours, writing text, code, highlights and cursor, then spacing and the Markdown preview. Backspace undoes.

Why these options (the research)
  • strong Dark text on a light background beat light-on-dark for acuity and proofreading, mostly because pupils get smaller, which also shrinks the halos LASIK and astigmatism cause. Biggest win for small text. Piepenbrock 2013, 2014.
  • strong Contrast: reading speed stays flat across a wide range and only collapses when text gets faint. So 10–15:1 is fine; below about 7:1 you pay. The floor here is 7:1. Legge 1987.
  • mixed Warm backgrounds (cream, peach) read slightly better than cool ones in one large study. That's why sepia and warm paper are in the theme round. Rello & Bigham 2017.
  • mixed Line length: people read ~100 characters fastest but prefer 45–72 and understand best around 55. Start: 80. Dyson 2004.
  • standard Line spacing 1.5× is the accessibility baseline. WCAG 1.4.12.
  • strong Size: at desk distance 16px is right at the floor for fluent reading; 18–20px leaves margin. Start for writing: 18px. Legge & Bigelow 2011.
  • strong Serif vs sans makes no difference; monospace is slightly slower for prose; and the best font differs a lot between people (up to 35% speed). That's why fonts are tested, not prescribed. Wallace 2022.
  • no evidence Named themes (iA Writer, Kindle sepia, GitHub, Solarized) have never been tested. They're in the theme round as starting points only.
